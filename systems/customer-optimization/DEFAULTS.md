# Customer Optimization Defaults

## Domain Boundary
Customer Optimization owns progression and mutually valuable value realization across the customer journey: conversion, purchase, onboarding, activation, success, retention, renewal, repeat, expansion, and referral. Customer Intelligence owns what customers believe and say; Marketing owns persuasion; Content owns communication production.

## Diagnosis
- Define the affected journey transition, baseline, expected customer state, expected business state, and measurable friction before choosing an intervention.
- Distinguish process/experience friction from persuasion, customer-understanding, product, sales, or technical problems and route to the correct owner.
- Use Customer Intelligence evidence such as reviews, sales/support/customer-success conversations, interviews, surveys, churn reasons, and complaints when they help explain a lifecycle mechanism; do not duplicate Customer Intelligence-owned interpretation.

## Customer and Business Value
- Do not optimize conversion, retention, expansion, or revenue in isolation. The desired relationship should create real customer value and sustainable business value.
- When definitions and data support them, consider realized customer outcomes, time-to-value, adoption, retention/renewal, repeat/expansion/referral, revenue, gross margin/contribution, cost-to-serve, LTV, CAC/payback, NRR/expansion, customer ROI/value realization, and value-at-risk. Unknown or inconsistently defined economics remain unknown.
- “High-value customer” must be contextually defined. High spend alone is not sufficient; include value realization/fit, durability, economics, support burden, and relationship quality where relevant.
- Do not use high account value as evidence that a customer is more likely to churn. Churn likelihood/risk indicators and consequence/value-at-risk are separate dimensions.
- Compare cohorts/segments with consistent eligibility/windows and account for mix, tenure, seasonality, censoring, survivorship, pricing/product changes, and attribution limits before inferring mechanisms.

## Intervention
Protect customer and business guardrails. Prefer controlled tests or staged rollout when causal uncertainty, scale, or reversibility makes broad deployment risky.
- Prioritize service recovery/value realization before premature upsell when unresolved failures or unmet customer outcomes make expansion inappropriate.
- Route persuasion/content/sales/product work through the correct owner rather than treating Customer Optimization as a universal revenue engine.

## Standalone Mode
When Customer Intelligence or other optional modules are absent, use journey data, direct customer evidence, Business Context, and bounded diagnosis sufficient for the active transition. Do not convert provisional customer interpretation into Customer Intelligence-owned canonical Insights.
