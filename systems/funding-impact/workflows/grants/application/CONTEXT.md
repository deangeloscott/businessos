# Grant Application

## Purpose
Write a grant application or letter of inquiry that answers what this funder asks for, in the funder's own terms, with evidence the organization can stand behind.

## Business Outcome
Higher award rates through funder-aligned, specific, credible applications, and better applications over time by learning from each decision.

## Run When
Use when the organization has chosen an opportunity to apply for, or must submit a letter of inquiry or full proposal. If fit is still uncertain, check it first with Grant Prospecting.

## Process
1. [AI] Read the funder's guidelines, scoring criteria, required sections, word and page limits, attachments, budget rules and deadline. Build a checklist of every requirement before drafting.
2. [AI] Identify what this funder cares about from its guidelines, priorities and past awards, and the two or three points the application must make convincing for this reviewer.
3. [AI] Draft from the organization's case for support and saved facts, tailored to the funder's language and priorities rather than generic text. For a portal or prescribed form, deliver paste-ready answers under the exact prompts, in order, and check each answer against its character or word limit. Use narrative sections where the funder allows them: summary, need (with sourced data), objectives that are specific and measurable, approach and timeline, organizational capacity, evaluation plan, sustainability beyond the grant where relevant, and budget with justification for each cost.
4. [HYBRID] Make sure objectives are measurable and the evaluation plan says how they will be measured; reuse the organization's impact measures where they exist. Keep every claim and number traceable; flag anything the organization must confirm.
5. [AI] Review against the requirement checklist and as a skeptical reviewer would: eligibility, alignment, clarity, feasibility, realistic budget and timeline, and anything that reads as boilerplate. Cross-check the same commitments across answers, work plan, evaluation, budget and attachments: participants served, staff hours, delivery dates, unit costs, request total and any required match. Resolve discrepancies or identify the specific figure or commitment the organization must confirm.
6. [HYBRID] For a drafting request, deliver the actual draft with any outstanding facts, approvals and attachments identified. At submission, the person submits through the funder's own process. Update the opportunity's status, save the submitted version, and note what was promised.
7. [DETERMINISTIC] When the decision arrives, record it (awarded, declined, amount, conditions) and any reviewer feedback. Ask for feedback after a decline when the funder allows it. Preserve Learning only when the evidence supports a reusable lesson about this funder or type of funder.

## Proportionate Scope
A short letter of inquiry needs a tight one or two pages. A large government or multi-year proposal may need a detailed plan, logic model and budget narrative. Match depth to the request and award size.

## Verification
- For a draft, every prompt has a usable answer or a clearly identified missing input. For a submission-ready packet, check required attachments, limits and totals against the current funder instructions.
- Objectives are measurable, the budget is realistic and justified, and claims trace to evidence.
- The application is clearly written for this funder, not recycled.
- For a submitted application, keep the version sent and submission confirmation when available. Record the decision and feedback when received.
