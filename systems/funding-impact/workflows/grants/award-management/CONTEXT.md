# Award Management and Reporting

## Purpose
Meet every obligation that comes with a grant, report results credibly, and turn the award into a lasting funder relationship.

## Business Outcome
No missed reports or compliance problems, funders who trust the organization, and a strong basis for renewal or the next request.

## Run When
Use when a grant is awarded, when a report or deliverable is coming due, and at close-out. Not needed for unrestricted gifts with no conditions.

## Process
1. [AI] Read the award letter, agreement and terms before acceptance where possible. Extract obligations: allowed and disallowed costs, restricted use, reporting dates and formats, deliverables, outcome measures promised, budget change rules, acknowledgment requirements, record keeping, and close-out steps.
2. [DETERMINISTIC] Create a dated obligations calendar with owners, and record each deadline where it will surface in time (for example as a scheduled reminder in the person's own tools or a collection with due dates).
3. [HYBRID] Confirm with the person how spending is tracked against the restricted budget. Financial compliance is the highest-risk area; involve the organization's finance lead or accountant for accounting questions rather than advising on them.
4. [AI] Track progress on the promised activities and outcomes using the organization's measurements, and flag early if something will be late, underspent or off-plan so the funder can be told before a report.
5. [AI] Write each report from recorded evidence: what was done, what changed, honest variances and why, what was learned, and stories with permission. Use the funder's format.
6. [AI] Keep the relationship warm beyond required reports: brief updates, invitations, and thanks.
7. [DETERMINISTIC] At close-out, complete final reports, record the outcome evaluation, and preserve Learning about delivery or the funder when supported. Note renewal timing.

## Proportionate Scope
A small foundation grant may need one short report. Government awards can carry detailed financial and compliance rules; read the governing terms carefully and match effort to the obligations.

## Verification
- Every obligation and deadline is listed with an owner and date.
- Reports match recorded evidence, explain variances honestly, and follow the funder's format.
- Accounting and legal questions are referred to qualified people rather than answered as advice.
