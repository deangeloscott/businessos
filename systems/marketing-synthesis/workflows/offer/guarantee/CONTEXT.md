# Offer Guarantee & Risk-Reversal Design

## Purpose
Determine whether legitimate risk reversal can remove a real purchase barrier and, when appropriate, design precise terms the organization could actually administer without presenting a proposed promise as current truth.

## Business Outcome
Reduce material buyer risk in a credible way while preserving customer fairness, operational feasibility, economics, qualification quality, and truthful marketing.

## Run When
Use when customer, sales, support, conversion, or other evidence indicates perceived risk is materially blocking qualified purchase and the Offer could realistically change.

## Process
1. Identify the exact perceived risk: outcome, implementation, time, quality, fit, switching, financial, contractual, effort, or another specific uncertainty. Do not default to a guarantee before understanding the actual barrier.
2. Resolve current Offer terms, economics, delivery variability, refund/cancellation policy, operational constraints, relevant ProofRecords, and applicable legal/compliance boundaries to the extent they materially affect the decision.
3. Consider the narrowest legitimate mechanism that directly addresses the risk: trial, milestone, conditional assurance, implementation commitment, cancellation flexibility, guarantee, qualification change, clearer proof/expectation setting, or another sound mechanism. A guarantee is one option, not the required output.
4. For candidate mechanisms, evaluate customer incentive effects, abuse risk, operational burden, financial downside, qualification consequences, fairness, and whether the business actually controls the promised condition.
5. If a guarantee or explicit assurance is justified, define candidate eligibility, customer obligations, covered outcome/milestone, evidence required, timeframe, remedy, exclusions, request process, and operational owner only to the detail needed for clear evaluation and administration.
6. Express the candidate in plain language that does not hide material conditions or negate the headline promise. Reject promises of outcomes outside the organization’s control or claims broader than available evidence supports.
7. Estimate economic exposure only from supported inputs. Keep unsupported expected/worst-case economics, abuse rates, or conversion lift explicitly unknown rather than manufacturing precision.
8. Recommend the narrowest credible option with exact proposed terms, rationale, material uncertainty, and useful guardrails or measurement when warranted. Keep proposed terms distinct from current Offer/BusinessClaim truth until the organization actually adopts them.
9. If adopted, persist the established Offer terms through the normal current-context path with provenance. If not adopted, preserve the candidate only when remembering it materially helps future work; do not manufacture an approval, WorkRequest, ContextUpdateProposal, or Experiment merely because the option was evaluated.

## Proportionate Scope
Investigate only the risks, economics, constraints, and operational details capable of changing the decision. Increase diligence when the proposed assurance creates substantial financial, legal, reputational, or delivery exposure.

## Verification
- The mechanism addresses an evidenced buyer risk rather than adding a generic guarantee for persuasion.
- Candidate wording is not treated as an approved outward claim before the organization establishes it.
- Economic, operational, and legal implications are supported or explicitly uncertain.
- No internal AURA approval lifecycle is required for ordinary evaluation or adoption by the real organizational owner.

## Completion Criteria
- The organization has either a precise evidence-aware risk-reversal proposal it can legitimately evaluate/adopt, or a supported conclusion that a guarantee/risk-reversal change is not the right intervention.
