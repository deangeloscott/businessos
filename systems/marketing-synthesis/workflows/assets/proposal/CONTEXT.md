# Client Proposal and RFP Response

## Purpose
Write a proposal for a specific client, or a response to a request for proposals (RFP), that wins because it answers the buyer's actual needs and requirements better than the alternatives.

## Business Outcome
More won work from proposals that are compliant, specific to the buyer, credible and easy to evaluate, with outcomes recorded so later proposals improve.

## Run When
Use when a specific prospect asks for a proposal, quote with approach, statement of work or bid, including public-sector RFPs. For reusable sales materials not tied to one buyer, use Sales Enablement.

## Process
1. [AI] Decide whether to bid: fit with the organization's offer and capacity, realistic chance against likely competitors, value, and effort. Declining a poor fit is a valid outcome.
2. [AI] For a formal RFP, build a requirements list first: every instruction, required section, evaluation criterion, page and format limit, attachment, certification and deadline, with where each will be answered. Update it as amendments or answers to questions arrive.
3. [AI] Understand the buyer's goals, problems, decision makers and evaluation criteria from the request, conversations and research. Identify two to four specific, evidence-backed reasons this organization is the best choice for this buyer, and carry them through every section.
4. [AI] Write in the buyer's terms: their situation and goals, the proposed approach and why it fits, scope and deliverables, timeline, team, relevant experience with case studies or references, pricing and terms, assumptions and exclusions, and clear next steps. Follow any required format exactly.
5. [HYBRID] Keep claims, results, references and pricing accurate and current; confirm pricing, terms and staffing commitments with the person. Do not provide legal advice on contract terms.
6. [AI] Review against the requirements list and as the evaluator would: compliance, clarity, specificity, price-to-value, and risks a buyer would worry about. Fix gaps before submission.
7. [DETERMINISTIC] After the decision, record the outcome, price, competitor if known, and any feedback, and preserve Learning when supported.

## Proportionate Scope
A small client may need a two-page proposal; a public RFP may need a long, strictly formatted response. Match depth to the opportunity and its evaluation rules.

## Verification
- Every stated requirement is met; nothing required is missing.
- The proposal is specific to this buyer, with claims and pricing confirmed.
- The outcome is recorded for future proposals.
