# Evidence Synthesis and Literature Review

## Purpose
Answer a research question from the body of available evidence: find the relevant studies and sources, assess them, and synthesize what they show together.

## Business Outcome
A trustworthy, sourced account of what is known, how strongly, where sources disagree, and what remains unknown, which the person can rely on for decisions, writing, grant applications or further research.

## Run When
Use when a question needs a review of many sources rather than one, such as a literature review, evidence brief, background section or state-of-knowledge summary. For judging a single study, use Research Quality Assessment; for ongoing monitoring of new research, use Research Evidence Review.

## Process
1. [AI] Agree the question and its boundaries with the person: population or subject, interventions or factors, comparisons, outcomes, time period, geography, and what the review is for. Decide the depth needed (a quick evidence brief, a rapid review, or a thorough review).
2. [AI] Plan and record the search: sources (academic databases, preprint servers, government and institutional reports, trusted datasets), search terms, date and language limits, and what will be included or excluded and why. State the shortcuts a faster review takes.
3. [INTEGRATION] Search, collect candidate sources, and screen them against the inclusion criteria. Keep track of what was found and why each was included or excluded.
4. [AI] Extract from each included source the key details: design, sample, setting, measures, findings and limitations. Save useful sources with their citation and a one-line finding.
5. [HYBRID] Assess the strength of evidence for each finding, using Research Quality Assessment where a study is pivotal. Weigh consistency across sources, study quality and relevance.
6. [AI] Synthesize across sources: where findings agree, where they conflict and possible reasons, how strong the overall evidence is for each conclusion, and the important gaps. Do not smooth disagreement into false consensus.
7. [AI] Write the review in the form the person needs, with citations for every claim, the method and its limitations stated, and conclusions no stronger than the evidence.

## Proportionate Scope
A quick brief may rest on a handful of strong sources found in an hour; a formal literature review needs a documented search and screening. Say which was done.

## Verification
- The question, search approach and inclusion rules are stated.
- Every claim cites its source; conflicting evidence is reported.
- Conclusions match the strength of the evidence and limitations are named.
