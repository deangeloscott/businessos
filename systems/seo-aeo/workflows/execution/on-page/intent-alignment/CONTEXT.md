# Intent Alignment

## Purpose
Align an existing page with the actual high-value user intent it should satisfy.

## Business Outcome
Make the destination better match what relevant users are trying to accomplish without blindly copying the current results or sacrificing legitimate differentiation.

## Use When
Use when a page's traffic, rankings, answer visibility, conversions, or observed result composition suggest that its current purpose or format may not match the demand it is intended to serve.

## Process
1. Define the target audience, awareness or buyer stage, demand cluster, page role, and intended business/customer outcome.
2. Inspect representative search/answer results and strong competing assets to understand the user task, expected information, and common asset types. Treat current winners as evidence, not a template that must be copied.
3. Compare the page's real purpose, information, proof, format, and next action with the observed intent and business objective.
4. Identify material mismatches such as wrong page type, missing decision information, over-broad focus, irrelevant sections, audience mismatch, or a destination that should serve a different intent.
5. Decide whether to rewrite, split, consolidate, retarget, redirect, or leave the page unchanged. Do not intervene when the existing differentiation is useful and the evidence for mismatch is weak.
6. If implementation is requested, preserve valuable existing coverage, links, evidence, and brand differentiation while making the smallest change that resolves the supported mismatch.
7. Re-evaluate the revised page against representative user tasks and factual truth before publication or deployment.

## Proportional Scope
Use enough competitive and demand evidence to understand the intent reliably. Expand when the result set is mixed, the stakes are high, or different markets/audiences could materially change the conclusion.
