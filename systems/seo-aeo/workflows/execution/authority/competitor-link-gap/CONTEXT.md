# Competitor Link Gap

## Purpose
Find relevant external sources that reference competitors, substitute solutions, or the topic but not the owned organization, then understand why those references exist.

## Business Outcome
Identify credible authority opportunities and asset gaps by learning from real editorial/reference patterns rather than copying competitor link counts or blindly prospecting shared domains.

## Use When
Use when competitor or category link evidence could reveal meaningful sources, reference-worthy asset types, relationship patterns, or authority gaps relevant to the business objective.

## Process
1. Select the competitors, substitutes, or benchmark organizations that genuinely matter to the current market, query set, audience, or authority question.
2. Collect and normalize the relevant linking or mentioning sources and pages, recognizing that backlink datasets are incomplete observations.
3. Filter sources by topical/business relevance, editorial legitimacy, audience fit, accessibility, and whether the organization could plausibly contribute real value.
4. Determine why the competitor earned the reference: original data, tool/resource value, expert insight, product utility, relationship, directory inclusion, newsworthiness, community relevance, or another real mechanism.
5. Match the observed mechanism to an appropriate strategy rather than treating every shared source as an outreach prospect. Sometimes the useful conclusion is to improve an asset, build a relationship, qualify for a directory, create original evidence, or do nothing.
6. Preserve a qualified Opportunity only when durable coordination is useful. Otherwise continue directly into the relevant asset, outreach, partnership, PR, or other work.

## Proportional Scope
Use the smallest competitor and source set that can reveal stable patterns for the current decision. Expand when the evidence is sparse, contradictory, strategically consequential, or likely to change the conclusion.
