# Video Continuity & Narrative

> Optional craft notes. Use this when a multi-shot idea needs a seam or story. A single clear shot may be the better answer.

## Keep the cut understandable

Generative or assembled sequences can lose product identity, reverse the action, change the light, or stop motion abruptly between shots. Carry only the continuity that the viewer needs:

1. **Cut on a useful action:** match a bottle tipping to the stream, a hand reaching to the latch, or a stride entering the next frame. A static cut can be better when it gives the viewer time to inspect.
2. **Keep a directional axis when it matters:** if the product or person travels left-to-right, preserve that relationship across the cut unless a deliberate reversal is part of the story.
3. **Carry the product and scene cues:** preserve supplied color, finish, label placement, proportions, key-light direction, and the relevant environment. Let the camera, crop, or dialect change when the story benefits.

Write these as ordinary scene instructions rather than a fixed scaffold. A source image, real clip, storyboard, 3D model, or edit can be more reliable than asking a model to bridge a difficult cut.

## An optional story rhythm

For a 10–30 second commercial, a useful rhythm is:

- **Hook:** introduce a friction, sensory detail, or striking situation.
- **Mechanism:** show the action or reason the product matters.
- **Use:** place it in a human workflow or meaningful habitat.
- **Resolve:** let the viewer read the product, result, offer, or next step.

These beats can happen in one shot, three shots, or a different order. Timing should follow the message and footage. A calm loop, direct demonstration, or single packshot may beat a four-beat story.

## Worked example: containment and relief

For a premium tumbler, one possible 12-second sequence is:

1. **Hook:** a hand turns the tumbler over a vulnerable surface; cut during the inversion only if the motion remains readable.
2. **Mechanism:** show the supplied lid construction or a clear close-up of the seal; an exploded view explains structure but does not prove airtight performance.
3. **Use:** place the tumbler on a counter and press the supplied closure with a visible, believable response.
4. **Resolve:** settle into a clean product view with the observed or clearly labeled conceptual result.

Swap in a real test, a generic contrast, or a different use scene when that better answers the buyer's question. Keep the test conditions and result separate from cinematic emphasis.
