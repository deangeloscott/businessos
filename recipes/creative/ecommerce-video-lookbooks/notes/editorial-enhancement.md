# Editorial Enhancement

> Optional craft notes for footage that already exists. Use them when a deliberate addition can improve understanding, feeling, identity, or rhythm; skip them when the cut is already doing the job.

## Start with the moment that needs help

Watch for a specific place where the viewer could lose the thread, miss a detail, need a stronger feeling, or benefit from a change of pace. Choose the smallest treatment that solves that problem. A graphic, crop, cutaway, caption, sound cue, color shift, or pause should earn its place by changing what the viewer understands or feels.

## Borrowable treatments

| If the moment needs… | Try… |
|---|---|
| **Clearer attention** | Crop toward the relevant detail, highlight the control or object, or let one short label arrive with the action. |
| **A clearer relationship** | Cut to the result, insert a diagram or screenshot, or let a simple graphic connect the cause to the consequence. |
| **More emotional weight** | Hold on a reaction, remove music for a beat, let room tone remain, or give the result enough stillness to register. |
| **More energy or humor** | Time a hard cut, oversized type, visual interruption, or sound accent to the exact word or action that earns it. |
| **A recognizable identity** | Reuse a restrained type treatment, color cue, framing habit, or sonic gesture at meaningful turns rather than decorating every shot. |
| **A stronger physical impression** | Pair a visible contact or change with a fitting sound, close crop, speed shift, or brief hold. |

For example, when a speaker describes three handoffs, let three labeled blocks appear beside the relevant actions. When the speaker explains the simpler path, have those blocks resolve into one clear route, then return attention to the footage. For a funny mistake, preserve the real reaction and let one precise caption or sound accent carry the joke instead of adding constant effects.

## Adapt to the material

Use the actual footage, transcript, product, and placement as the source of truth. Let an overlay follow the action it explains; keep a crop, caption, or graphic away from the face, product detail, or evidence the viewer needs to inspect. A cutaway can show a consequence, context, or human response, but it should not quietly replace a missing demonstration. If audio may be unavailable, keep essential meaning visible through the footage or captions and use sound for reinforcement, texture, or mood.

Graphics can be explanatory, emotional, playful, or identity-building. Motion can be restrained or bold. Color can mark a change, not merely make every shot louder. Silence can be the most direct way to make a line or action land. Choose the treatment that serves the moment and remove it when it competes with it.

The [motion graphics starting points](../../motion-graphics/INDEX.md) supply concrete overlays, diagrams, comparisons, and transitions. The [shared audio starting points](../../audio/INDEX.md) cover narration, music, Foley, sonic identity, and silence. Use the [continuity and narrative notes](continuity-and-narrative.md) when an addition crosses a cut, and the [storyboard workflow](../../../../systems/content-synthesis/workflows/production/storyboard/CONTEXT.md) when the relationships between seen, heard, and read elements need to be planned.

There is no required effect stack or decoration quota. If the footage already communicates the point, leave it alone. If a chosen treatment does not improve comprehension, emotion, rhythm, or identity at the target size, simplify or remove it.
