# Editorial Explainer Starting Points

> Two adaptable concepts for explaining a system, cause, or consequence through footage and graphics. They are borrowable structures, not required scene lists; a short piece may need only one clear visual move.

## Follow the hidden path

Follow one object, request, payment, decision, or piece of information through the places and people it touches. Begin with the thing the viewer recognizes, then reveal the handoff or relationship that is hard to see. Return to the original subject with a clearer understanding of what changed.

For a delivery delay, start with a waiting customer, follow the package across a simple route or sequence of handoffs, and return to the moment the delay affects the customer. For a software service, follow one request from intake to resolution. For a physical product, follow a material or component from source to finished use when that journey is relevant and documented.

Use real footage for the human or physical situation, a map or diagram when the relationship is spatial or procedural, and typography only where it helps the viewer keep the thread. The [mechanism graphic](../../motion-graphics/05-make-the-mechanism-clear.md), [route or coverage graphic](../../motion-graphics/09-show-place-route-or-coverage.md), and [clear spoken explanation](../../audio/01-clear-spoken-explanation.md) are optional pieces to borrow. Keep the path as simple as the viewer's question allows.

## Reveal the hidden cause

Begin with a visible symptom or a question the viewer can recognize, then expose the invisible relationship that produces it. A cutaway, magnified detail, simple diagram, or stylized visualization can make the cause legible before the piece returns to the real-world result.

To explain condensation, begin with droplets forming on a cold glass, move through a clear cutaway showing warm, moist air meeting the cooled surface, and return to the glass to show the practical consequence for the hand or table. Treat the cutaway as an explanation; use supplied product or material facts when the piece makes a specific performance claim. The same idea can reveal heat moving through a package, pressure changing inside a mechanism, or a signal becoming an audible result.

The question may be spoken, shown as type, or implied by the opening image. The answer may be a demonstration, diagram, macro detail, interview, or real example. Use the [statement treatment](../../motion-graphics/02-make-a-statement-land.md), [state-change graphic](../../motion-graphics/03-show-the-change.md), and [spoken explanation](../../audio/01-clear-spoken-explanation.md) when they fit. The [storyboard workflow](../../../../systems/content-synthesis/workflows/production/storyboard/CONTEXT.md) can map which medium carries each beat; it does not require every medium to appear.

Both concepts can be quiet, investigative, persuasive, humorous, or highly graphic. Adapt the voice, evidence, pacing, and ending to the audience and purpose. If the footage or a single diagram already answers the question, use that and stop.
