# Video idea: Texture in motion

> A quick, adaptable starting point for making a product's texture and use feel tangible. Borrow the idea, replace the product and action, combine it with another recipe, or skip it when you already have a better plan. The cosmetic example below is one execution, not a required script or an absorption claim.

**Job:** Sensory ritual · [micro/intimate scale](../../ecommerce-image-lookbooks/01-micro-intimate.md) · useful for a short social hook, product page loop, or brand detail.

## The idea

Show one satisfying physical transformation: dispense the product, bring it into contact with a suitable surface, then spread, settle, melt, or finish in a way that lets the viewer imagine its texture. Keep the frame close enough to read the material and calm enough that the action carries the scene.

For a serum, that might be a suspended drop and one glide. For a cream, use a squeeze or scoop and a deliberate sweep. For a balm, show a clean pick-up and melt. A visual finish can be aspirational; describe it as a concept unless the supplied product sample or record supports a factual result.

## One concrete execution

The worked example is a 4-second vertical close-up of an amber dropper bottle above a forearm. A locked macro camera holds the contact area while the pipette releases one viscous golden drop, the drop lands and spreads, and the shot settles on the supplied or clearly labeled conceptual finish. A simple arc works well:

1. **Position:** 0.0–0.8s, bring the applicator into place.
2. **Contact:** 0.8–2.8s, slow the release and first spread so thickness, surface tension, and contact are readable.
3. **Settle:** 2.8–4.0s, let the product and camera quiet down on a clean end state.

Use a supplied start/end image, a single reference frame, real footage, or a storyboard only when that method fits the tool. Keep the bottle, label, applicator, skin, and product contact continuous. A light glint on the drop and a soft reflection that widens across the surface can make the material read; they are visual cues, not proof of hydration, absorption, safety, or residue.

## Adapt it

Choose the surface, action, tempo, and framing from the actual product and audience. A calm longer loop, a one-shot squeeze, or a fast social cut may be better than this four-second arc. If exact packaging matters, supply a reference image and inspect label, hand, skin, droplet trajectory, and phase-through in the finished render.

## Optional shot brief

```text
Create a short close-up of [PRODUCT] using one clear tactile action: [DISPENSE / SQUEEZE / SCOOP / POUR] onto [SURFACE], followed by [ONE APPLICATION OR SPREAD]. Let the action reveal [TEXTURE / FINISH] with lighting that makes the material readable. Preserve the supplied product identity and use a simple position → contact → settle rhythm. Choose framing, duration, and speed for [PLACEMENT]. Treat any unverified finish as a visual concept and inspect geometry, contact, hands, and label continuity.
```
