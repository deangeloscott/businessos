# Optional Brand Dialects
### Cross-cutting aesthetic modifiers for e-commerce archetypes
*Version 2.2 — Sixteen optional brand styling and art-direction lenses*

---

## 1. What Is a Brand Dialect?

A **Brand Dialect** is an optional aesthetic modifier. While the Scale Archetypes (`01` through `06`) define the **spatial and functional setup** (for example, a tabletop scene, macro close-up, apparel movement shot, or architectural patio), a Brand Dialect suggests mood, lighting, material palette, color grade, and cultural attitude.

Apply only the parts that improve the communication job. A dialect may shape light, stage, styling, typography, motion, or post-production without overruling product facts, accessibility, platform requirements, or the viewer's need to understand the product. It does not require a particular prompt syntax, fixed contrast ratio, or universal prop list. If a dialect's aesthetic conflicts with label legibility, scale, or a supported claim, keep the communication requirement and adapt the aesthetic.

Any archetype in this library can be viewed through any dialect lens, when the combination serves the brief:
* *Scale 02 Archetype 03 (Kinetic Pour)* + **Dialect 02: Playful Utility** = Saturated morning sun, olive oil splatters, vibrant yellow linen napkin, candid kitchen energy.
* *Scale 02 Archetype 12 (Viscosity Flow)* + **Dialect 03: Quiet Luxury** = Muted chiaroscuro, unsealed limestone, raw linen, single ceramic object, silent contemplative tone.
* *Scale 02 Archetype 12 (Viscosity Flow)* + **Dialect 13: Solar Mediterranean Riviera** = High-noon terracotta patio, golden sun glare, glistening sea-salt crystals, sun-warmed indulgent energy.

### Dialect 01: Sensorial Glow
* **Iconic Benchmarks:** *Rhode, Dieux Skin, Glossier, Augustinus Bader.*
* **Brand Attitude:** Skin-literate, ritualistic, effortless, dewy, self-care intimate.
* **Lighting signature:**
  * High-key, ultra-soft directional skylight or massive 6-foot diffused scrim.
  * Low contrast ratio (2:1); no harsh black shadows.
  * Pronounced, luminous specular highlights on dewy/wet surfaces.
  * Color temperature: 5200K clean neutral daylight with a whisper of soft pearl warmth.
* **Surface and material palette:**
  * Frosted glass, translucent acrylic blocks, bone/cream plaster, brushed pale aluminum, soft draped silk or micro-terrycloth.
* **Color Grading:**
  * Monochromatic neutrals: ecru, bone, warm ivory, whisper pink, translucent sage. Zero harsh primary colors.
* **Human styling:**
  * Fresh, bare, hydrated skin with visible pores and natural glow (zero foundation cake). Casual undone hair, minimalist neutral manicures, natural relaxed hands.
* **Anti-patterns to avoid:**
  * Hard black shadows, oversaturated primary colors, matte powder dryness, heavy makeup.

### Dialect 02: Playful Utility (The "Countertop Flex")
* **Iconic Benchmarks:** *Graza, Fly By Jing, Fishwife, Mid-Day Squares.*
* **Brand Attitude:** Unfussy foodie culture, culinary joy, bold utility, countertop pride, anti-snob.
* **Lighting signature:**
  * Direct hard sunlight at a 45° angle or punchy on-camera studio flash.
  * Sharp, crisp, graphic cast shadows with clean edge definition.
  * Vivid highlights that accentuate liquids, glazes, and packaging gloss.
  * Color temperature: 5600K bright summer noon sun.
* **Surface and material palette:**
  * Stainless steel prep tables, butcher block wood, glazed retro subway tile, checkerboard linoleum, butcher paper.
  * *Props:* Real kitchen crumbs, garlic peels, lemon halves, drips, vintage glassware, bold diner plates.
* **Color Grading:**
  * High saturation, vibrant primary and secondary pops: electric yellow, olive green, cherry red, cobalt blue. Clean whites with warm highlights.
* **Human styling:**
  * Active hands mid-cook (rolling dough, squeezing bottles, slicing bread). Natural un-manicured hands, rolled-up cotton sleeves, effortless everyday joy.
* **Anti-patterns to avoid:**
  * Sterile medical cleanliness, muted grey luxury palettes, stiff formal staging.

### Dialect 03: Quiet Luxury & Architectural Chiaroscuro
* **Iconic Benchmarks:** *Aesop, Le Labo, Frama, Studio Nicholson, The Row.*
* **Brand Attitude:** Monastic, architectural, contemplative, timeless, sensory restraint.
* **Lighting signature:**
  * High-contrast chiaroscuro (key-to-fill ratio of 6:1 or 8:1).
  * Single directional window or narrow snooted spotlight casting long, sculptural, soft-edged shadows.
  * Mood: Intentionally underexposed ambient background (-1 to -1.5 EV) allowing the hero product to emerge from shadow.
  * Color temperature: 3200K–4000K warm incandescent or golden afternoon gloom.
* **Surface and material palette:**
  * Unsealed honed travertine, raw volcanic stone, distressed blackened steel, lime plaster walls, aged dark walnut, heavy textured Belgian linen.
* **Color Grading:**
  * Desaturated, earthy, tonal neutrals: umber, charcoal, raw taupe, espresso, chalk.
* **Human styling:**
  * Solitary, contemplative postures. Architectural silhouettes, sculptural jewelry, heavy wool or crisp poplin garments.
* **Anti-patterns to avoid:**
  * High-key white backgrounds, bubbly or cheerful props, bright neon colors, casual clutter.

### Dialect 04: Industrial Brutalism & Technical Precision
* **Iconic Benchmarks:** *Teenage Engineering, Nothing, Peak Design, Ridge, Arc'teryx Veilance.*
* **Brand Attitude:** Engineered, utilitarian, radical transparency, modular, tactical.
* **Lighting signature:**
  * Surgical rim lighting and cross-polarized linear LED strips.
  * Hard specular highlights reflecting along aluminum chamfers, knurled knobs, and screw heads.
  * Pure neutral ambient fill; zero color cast.
  * Color temperature: 5500K calibrated industrial white.
* **Surface and material palette:**
  * Anodized aluminum plates, anti-static cutting mats, perforated pegboards, raw concrete slabs, smoked acrylic, precision grid paper.
* **Color Grading:**
  * Deep technical black (RGB 15,15,15), cool gunmetal greys, stark industrial white, punctuated by a single functional accent color (safety orange, signal yellow, or cyan).
* **Human styling:**
  * Precision hand movements (pressing switches, adjusting dials, unlatching carabiners). Functional workwear, tactical nylon, unpretentious craft focus.
* **Anti-patterns to avoid:**
  * Soft romantic domesticity, pastel tones, rustic wood, messy organic crumbs or plants.

## 3. Borrow a dialect when it helps

When a structured brief is useful, borrow only the dialect ingredients that sharpen the job: its light, surface, palette, styling, copy space, motion, or finish. Keep the product's supplied dimensions, materials, label orientation, and functional communication requirement in view. A different order, a hybrid dialect, a native tool control, or no dialect at all may be better.

After generation, inspect product identity, dimensions, label text, material behavior, human anatomy, contrast, and crop safety. A beautiful dialect cannot convert an unsupported performance statement into proof; use supplied references or a documented demonstration for that.
