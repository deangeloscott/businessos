# Guide the Eye Through a Demo

Make the important action and its result easy to follow in a screen recording, product demonstration, or piece of footage.

## Borrow this treatment

Use one restrained guide at the moment attention could be lost: a cursor, ring, crop, label, underline, magnified detail, or short connector line. Let it arrive from the direction of the action, point to the relevant control or physical detail, and leave once the viewer has the connection. Keep the action or object that matters as the focal element while the guide moves around it. Pair the guide with a clear action-to-result relationship instead of decorating the whole frame.

## Adapt it

Use the supplied interface, footage, or product reference as the source of truth. Choose a pointer, highlight, or callout that stays legible at the final size and does not cover the behavior being explained. A real screen capture, annotated still, live presenter, or simple cut may be more reliable than generated motion for a precise interface. Keep the visible action understandable without relying only on color or sound.

## Example

In a checkout demo, let the cursor select the relevant option, briefly label what changes, and hold the resulting total or state. In a hardware demo, use a small callout to connect a hand movement with the latch, dial, or interface response that matters.

## Try it when

The viewer needs help finding one control, detail, or consequence inside a busy visual.
