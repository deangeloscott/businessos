# Make One Number Matter

Turn a figure that could be skimmed into a relationship the viewer can understand.

## Borrow this treatment

Give one number a clear moment on screen, then show its scale, change, or consequence with a second visual: a bar reaching a previous mark, recognizable units accumulating, a timeline compressing, or two quantities sharing the same frame. Keep the number, unit, comparison, and source relationship together so the animation explains rather than decorates. Let the reveal settle long enough to read.

## Adapt it

Use a documented result, price, date, capacity, duration, or count. Choose the comparison that answers the viewer's natural question: compared with what, over which period, or enough for whom? If the exact figure is not established, make the piece qualitative or label it as a concept. A number can be the whole graphic, a beat inside a video, or a small data moment in a presentation.

## Example

For a service with a documented 48-hour turnaround, divide one timeline into two equal day blocks, move a marker from request to delivery, and let the 48-hour figure resolve beside the same scale. For a product with a known capacity, fill a set of familiar units until the stated capacity is reached. The visual makes the relationship clear without adding a claim the source does not support.

## Try it when

The audience needs help interpreting a result, comparison, or scale—not merely seeing a large numeral.
