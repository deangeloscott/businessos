# Put a Person or Quote in Context

Give a speaker, customer, expert, or quoted idea enough context to be understood without competing with what they are saying.

## Borrow this treatment

Introduce the name, role, source, or short quote at the moment it becomes useful. A lower third can enter from the speaker's visual direction and settle in a quiet area; a quote can build around one phrase while keeping the complete meaning available. Use a portrait, source mark, or small visual cue only when it helps orientation. Let the context leave once the viewer has it.

## Adapt it

Use supplied names, roles, quotations, and permissions. Keep a quote faithful to its source and distinguish a customer statement from organizational copy. The treatment can support interviews, testimonials, expert clips, case stories, webinars, presentations, or a text-only quote card. Use captions or a written alternative where the spoken content is essential and sound is uncertain.

## Example

For an interview clip, let the speaker's name and role appear beside the first useful statement, then reduce the label while the quote remains. For a customer comment, show a concise excerpt with the customer's actual context and source rather than turning an isolated sentence into a broader promise.

## Try it when

The audience needs to know who is speaking, why the source matters, or which words are a quote before the content can carry its full weight.
