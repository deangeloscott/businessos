# Compare Two Choices Clearly

Make a meaningful difference between products, plans, approaches, or states visible at a glance.

## Borrow this treatment

Choose one comparison question and give both sides the same visual frame: side by side, a matched sequence, a split state, or a focused comparison table. Reveal the shared dimension first, then emphasize the difference the audience needs to evaluate. Keep labels, units, conditions, and time periods close to the things they describe so the graphic does not turn a selective contrast into a false equivalence.

## Adapt it

Use a documented comparison when the asset makes a factual claim. A generic best-versus-poor contrast can illustrate a quality spectrum, but make it clear when the weaker side is illustrative rather than a measured competitor or test result. Compare one or two decision-relevant dimensions rather than filling the frame with every feature. The same idea can explain plans, workflows, materials, service levels, or before-and-after choices.

## Example

For two service plans, hold the audience's shared need in the center, then animate the differences in included help, response window, or usage limit from supplied terms. For a product ad, show a meaningful construction or use difference through a matched detail rather than labeling one side “better” without showing why.

## Try it when

The viewer is weighing alternatives and a list of isolated benefits would make the decision harder to see.
