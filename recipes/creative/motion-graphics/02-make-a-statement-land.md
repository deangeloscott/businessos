# Make a Statement Land

Give a headline, quote, promise, announcement, or call to action a visual beat that makes its meaning easy to hold onto.

## Borrow this treatment

Start with the complete thought or its most important phrase. Bring words on in a way that follows the sentence's emphasis: a quiet setup, one change in scale or position, then a clean hold on the line that matters. Use line breaks, weight, contrast, or a single graphic mark to show hierarchy. A lower third, quote card, or end card can use the same idea when the text has a clear job.

## Adapt it

Use the audience's own language when it is available, or write original copy that fits the actual offer and evidence. Keep the readable phrase short enough for the placement and support it with captions or an alternate text treatment where needed. The motion can be calm, abrupt, editorial, playful, or restrained; choose the rhythm for the message rather than adding movement to every word.

## Example

For a product reveal, let a small line establish the problem, then expand the product's one meaningful difference into the visual center. For an interview clip, hold the speaker's complete quote and emphasize the phrase that carries the insight without changing its meaning.

## Try it when

The message is strong but is being lost in a busy frame, a long sentence, or a feed where the first readable idea must arrive quickly.
