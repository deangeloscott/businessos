# Show Place, Route, or Coverage

Make location, movement through space, service coverage, or geographic context easy to picture.

## Borrow this treatment

Start with the place or area that matters, then reveal one route, boundary, cluster, or change at a time. Use a simple map, floor plan, satellite-style illustration, or spatial diagram according to the audience's need. Keep the destination, direction, scale, labels, and timing stable enough that the viewer can follow the movement without decoding the graphic.

## Adapt it

Use current supplied locations, routes, coverage areas, and boundaries. If the map is illustrative or simplified, label it as such and avoid implying precise availability or distance. This can explain a delivery area, event path, travel story, service footprint, facility layout, logistics flow, or a product's place in a real environment. A static diagram may be better when the route itself is not changing.

## Example

For a local service, reveal the operating area and then highlight the customer's path from request to arrival. For an event, introduce the venue, animate the route between key points, and bring the next action into the same spatial frame.

## Try it when

The viewer's question is “where?”, “how do I get there?”, “what area does this cover?”, or “how does this move through the space?”
