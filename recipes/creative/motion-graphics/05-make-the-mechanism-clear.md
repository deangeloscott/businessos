# Make the Mechanism Clear

Explain how a product, process, system, or service produces an outcome that is difficult to see directly.

## Borrow this treatment

Build the explanation in causal order. Introduce the input, reveal the important parts or stages, connect them with movement or labels, and finish at the observable result. Use a simple diagram, exploded view, path, sequence, or metaphor that preserves the relationship the viewer actually needs to understand. Remove detail that does not help the explanation.

## Adapt it

Use documented product structure, process steps, or source material for factual diagrams. Simplify the visual while keeping the meaning honest. If an internal mechanism is unknown, show a clearly illustrative model or stay at the level of the user-visible experience. The same idea can explain a physical device, a software pipeline, a service handoff, a supply chain, or a scientific concept.

## Example

For a delivery service, move an order through the few stages that explain the customer's experience, then connect the final handoff to the promised action. For a device, reveal the supplied components and show the path that leads to the visible output rather than adding impressive but unverified internals.

## Try it when

The audience needs to understand why something works, not only what it looks like or what the headline promises.
