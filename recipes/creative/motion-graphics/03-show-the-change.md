# Show the Change

Make a before-and-after, feature reveal, workflow, or problem-to-relief story visible as a change of state.

## Borrow this treatment

Establish the starting state, perform one meaningful action, and let the resolved state replace or grow from it. A wipe, transform, rearrangement, highlight, or direct cut can work; the best choice is the one that preserves the relationship between the two states. Keep the subject, frame, and labels stable when that makes the difference easier to understand. If the scene changes, carry one important object, label, or edge through the transition so the viewer has something to follow.

## Adapt it

Use a real before and after when the work represents an observed result. If the piece is a concept, product preview, or illustrative metaphor, make that status clear and do not let a polished transition imply an outcome that has not been established. A direct cut can make a decisive contrast feel immediate, a continuous transform can make causality easy to follow, and a crossfade can suit a reflective change; choose the treatment that explains the relationship. The recipe can show a UI state change, a physical setup, a service journey, a layout improvement, or a product feature.

## Example

For a software feature, begin with a crowded report, let the relevant filter or action appear, and resolve into the focused view the user wanted. For a physical product, show the awkward starting setup and one action that produces the intended usable arrangement. The motion gives the viewer the causal bridge instead of placing two unrelated stills side by side.

## Try it when

The benefit is a difference between states and a static benefit list would make the relationship harder to picture.
