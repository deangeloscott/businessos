# Pitch or proposal deck

Use this when a presentation needs to help a prospective customer, partner, funder, or internal sponsor understand an opportunity and decide whether to proceed.

## The idea

Make the recipient's situation the center of the story. Establish the opportunity or problem, show the proposed response, make the value and evidence concrete, explain what participation requires, and end with a specific decision. A proposal should make the choice easier to evaluate, not bury it under company history.

## Borrowable shape

**Recipient's situation → meaningful opportunity or problem → proposed approach → why this approach → evidence and fit → scope, terms, and tradeoffs → ask and next step.**

Use the recipient's language and actual constraints. Show what is included, what is assumed, what remains unknown, and what the next decision unlocks. Keep generic credentials or decorative slides after the material that answers “why this, why now, and what happens next?”

## Tiny illustrative example

**A regional retailer is considering a service to reduce stockout surprises.**

The deck opens with the retailer's current inventory question, demonstrates the proposed replenishment view, shows the information required to start, and closes with a scoped decision. Replace any results, customers, price, timeline, or guarantee with verified proposal inputs.

## Adapt

For a sales proposal, make fit and commercial terms easy to compare. For an internal pitch, make the decision, owner, resource need, and downside visible. For a partnership, show the exchange and responsibilities on both sides. If the audience already knows the problem, shorten the context and spend more time on the decision criteria and next action.
