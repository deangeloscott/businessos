# Short recommendation deck

Use this when a presentation needs to help a person or group understand a choice and leave with a clear next step.

## The idea

Build a small narrative around a decision rather than a tour of everything known. Establish the question, make the relevant context visible, compare the viable paths, recommend one, and name the tradeoff or next action. A short deck can be three slides or several more; the decision should determine the length.

## Borrowable shape

**Decision to make → context and criteria → options → recommendation → tradeoff, owner, and next step.**

Give each slide one job and carry the same terms across the deck. Put detail in speaker notes or an appendix when it is useful but not necessary for the decision. If the evidence is incomplete, show the uncertainty and frame the recommendation as a choice under current assumptions.

## Tiny illustrative example

**A local event team is choosing how to introduce a new community workshop.**

1. **Question:** Which launch path gives the team the clearest first signal?
2. **Options:** A small invite-only session or a broad public release.
3. **Recommendation:** Start with the smaller session if the team needs to learn the format first; name the reach tradeoff plainly.
4. **Next step:** Confirm the audience, learning signal, owner, and review date.

The example demonstrates the narrative; the actual recommendation depends on the event's evidence and constraints.

## Adapt

Replace the fictional decision with the actual one, use evidence the audience can inspect, and choose the smallest deck that lets them act. For a live presentation, keep the slides spare and let the speaker carry nuance; for a leave-behind, add the labels, assumptions, and source context a reader will need without narration. A product presentation can use the same arc to move from buyer question to recommendation; a strategy review can use it to surface assumptions and open decisions. If no decision is needed, use a simpler narrative or one-page graphic instead.
