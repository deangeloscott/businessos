# Annotated detail or mechanism

> Optional craft reference. Use this when a viewer needs to understand how something is assembled, connected, moves, or produces an effect. Borrow the level of detail; use a whole scene, simpler diagram, or another method when that communicates better.

## Start with the question

Name the detail that earns attention: which part matters, how do parts relate, what changes, or why does an outcome follow? Keep enough of the whole object, scene, or system to orient the viewer before enlarging the part that answers the question.

## Choose the representation

- **Source-faithful technical:** use supplied drawings, photographs, CAD, measurements, screenshots, or other references when geometry, scale, components, or labels matter. Preserve what is known and mark what is not.
- **Explanatory simplification:** remove irrelevant parts, enlarge a relationship, or use a cutaway so the mechanism can be understood. Preserve the relationship or sequence, but do not imply that the simplified view is a literal record.
- **Speculative or conceptual:** show a possible mechanism, future design, or invisible process when the source does not establish the internals. Label it as an illustration or concept rather than observed evidence.

## Borrowable shape

**Whole or orientation → meaningful detail or cutaway → anchored labels and arrows → relationship or short sequence → implication.**

Anchor an arrow to the component or action it describes. Use verbs such as **enters**, **transfers**, **seals**, **turns**, or **exits** when direction matters; reserve legends for symbols the viewer must decode. Label only what changes understanding, keep leader lines from crossing when possible, and use a second view or a short series when one frame would make the detail unreadable.

### Tiny illustrative example

For a technical explainer about heat in a power unit, show the whole unit first, enlarge the supplied heat-producing and heat-releasing parts, and trace a simple path such as **generated → conducted → released**. If the internals are not documented, use a generic simplified illustration and identify it as conceptual rather than implying a teardown or measured result.

## Adapt to the medium

Use a supplied photograph, drawing, vector, 3D view, annotated screenshot, generated illustration, or edited sequence according to the question and available source. A single image can orient and explain; a series can preserve orientation while revealing stages or layers. Design the important relationship for the intended crop and size, not for an uncropped master frame.

Keep labels separate and editable when revision, translation, or localization is likely. Baked-in text may match a fixed composition quickly, but it trades away easy correction and language variants. Judge the result by whether the viewer can identify the whole, the detail, and the relationship without inventing missing evidence; simplify, enlarge, or change format when they cannot.
