# One number that carries the message

Use this when one verified figure can make the central point easier to understand than a paragraph or a dense chart.

## The idea

Give the number a clear job. State the takeaway in the headline, show the figure large, and supply just enough context for a reader to interpret it correctly. A number without a period, denominator, comparison, or source can create more confusion than clarity.

## Borrowable shape

**Takeaway headline → one prominent figure → what it measures and when → comparison or implication → source and proportionate next action.**

Use a before-and-after, a rate with its denominator, a count with its scope, or a time value with its unit when that context changes the meaning. Keep the supporting source close to the figure. If the number is not verified, use a qualitative statement or label the graphic as a concept.

## Tiny illustrative example

**Show one verified time figure with its meaning attached.**

An illustrative card places **“8 min”** in the center, with the caption **“time to complete setup”** and a source line reserved for the actual measurement. Replace the sample number with a supported figure or remove it before publication.

## Adapt

Choose the figure that answers the audience's real question: how much, how long, how often, how many, or what changed. Use a chart when relationships or uncertainty matter more than one headline number. Let the visual scale reflect the value honestly, and never make a sample number, estimate, or target look like a measured result.
