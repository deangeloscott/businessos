# One-page executive brief

Use this when a busy reader needs the current situation, the important implication, and the decision or action in one skimmable page.

## The idea

Put the conclusion near the top, then give only the context required to trust and act on it. An executive brief is a compact decision surface, not a compressed archive of every finding. It should make the open question, recommendation, evidence, uncertainty, and owner easy to find.

## Borrowable shape

**Purpose or decision → takeaway → supporting facts → implications and risks → recommendation → owner and next step.**

Use headings, short paragraphs, and a small number of purposeful visual blocks. Separate observed facts from interpretation and from a proposed choice. Link to the underlying report, source, or appendix when the reader needs depth.

## Tiny illustrative example

**Decision needed: choose the next customer-research focus.**

**Takeaway:** The current notes describe interest but do not yet distinguish the two most plausible use cases. **Recommendation:** interview a small set of people from each use case before changing the offer. **Next step:** assign an owner, define the questions, and return with the comparison.

Use actual notes and evidence in the final brief.

## Adapt

Use the smallest set of supported facts that changes the decision. Put caveats beside the claim they qualify, not in a distant footnote. If there is no decision or action, an insight summary or report page may be a better format. If the audience needs to persuade others, turn the same material into a short recommendation or proposal deck.
