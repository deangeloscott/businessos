# Editorial Enhancement

> Optional craft notes for footage that already exists. Use them when a deliberate cut, change, or addition can improve understanding, feeling, identity, or rhythm; skip them when the cut is already doing the job.

## Start with the moment that needs help

Watch for a specific place where the viewer could lose the thread, miss a detail, need a stronger feeling, or benefit from a change of pace. Choose the smallest treatment that solves that problem. A graphic, crop, cutaway, caption, sound cue, color shift, or pause should earn its place by changing what the viewer understands or feels.

## Borrowable treatments

| If the moment needs… | Try… |
|---|---|
| **Clearer attention** | Crop toward the relevant detail, highlight the control or object, or let one short label arrive with the action. |
| **A clearer relationship** | Cut to the result, insert a diagram or screenshot, or let a simple graphic connect the cause to the consequence. |
| **More emotional weight** | Hold on a reaction, remove music for a beat, let room tone remain, or give the result enough stillness to register. |
| **More energy or humor** | Time a hard cut, oversized type, visual interruption, or sound accent to the exact word or action that earns it. |
| **A recognizable identity** | Reuse a restrained type treatment, color cue, framing habit, or sonic gesture at meaningful turns rather than decorating every shot. |
| **A stronger physical impression** | Pair a visible contact or change with a fitting sound, close crop, speed shift, or brief hold. |

For example, when a speaker describes three handoffs, let three labeled blocks appear beside the relevant actions. When the speaker explains the simpler path, have those blocks resolve into one clear route, then return attention to the footage. For a funny mistake, preserve the real reaction and let one precise caption or sound accent carry the joke instead of adding constant effects.

## Cut recorded speech without changing the record

For talking-heads, interviews, and voiceover, trim dead air, false starts, repetitions, or mistakes when that improves clarity, rhythm, or intended delivery; keep pauses and reactions that carry meaning or character. In conversation, keep speaker attribution and turn-taking clear; do not make a person appear to answer words, timing, or emotion they did not receive. Correcting transcript or caption spelling, punctuation, attribution, or timing is not rewriting the spoken record.

Transcription is optional: a transcript can help locate an excerpt, plan an extraction, or prepare captions, but it never replaces listening to the recording. Retime and proof captions against the final cut. If a line is missing or unusable, an alternate take, pickup, voiceover, or permitted synthetic edit may help. Keep transcript corrections distinct from changes to the performance; when source authenticity matters, do not present a replacement as evidence of what was originally said.

## Keep action, coverage, and context honest

For tutorials, preserve the action-to-result connection: keep the relevant control, state, and result continuous, or use B-roll, an insert, screen capture, diagram, split screen, or picture-in-picture that genuinely shows or supports the relationship. Let A-roll carry the through-line when useful; B-roll and support views can clarify or cover a purposeful cut, not conceal a missing step or imply an unshown result.

Choose full-frame, split-screen, picture-in-picture, crop, or another layout by what must remain readable—face, control, product, caption, and evidence—not by a fixed ratio. Adapt the support view to the source and placement; do not force a split or keep changing layouts when a single view is clearer. A standalone excerpt needs enough setup, qualifiers, attribution, and conclusion to make sense; add brief context or keep it in the parent piece.

## Treat audio problems specifically

Fix the problem you can hear. Level normalization changes loudness; it does not denoise, de-reverb, or repair distortion. Match levels across speakers without flattening natural dynamics, preserve sync, and keep room tone coherent across cuts. Use noise reduction, repair, or enhancement only when the defect is present and the treatment preserves intelligibility and naturalness. Restoration cannot establish unheard words or recover a source event that did not occur. If the recording is insufficient, use another take or an intentional production edit; preserve the distinction when the piece presents the recording as factual evidence.

## Adapt to the material

Use the actual footage, transcript, product, and placement as the source of truth. Let an overlay follow the action it explains; keep a crop, caption, or graphic away from the face, product detail, or evidence the viewer needs to inspect. A cutaway can show a consequence, context, or human response, but it should not quietly replace a missing demonstration. If audio may be unavailable, keep essential meaning visible through the footage or captions and use sound for reinforcement, texture, or mood.

Graphics can be explanatory, emotional, playful, or identity-building. Motion can be restrained or bold. Color can mark a change, not merely make every shot louder. Silence can be the most direct way to make a line or action land. Choose the treatment that serves the moment and remove it when it competes with it.
