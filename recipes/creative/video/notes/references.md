## Selected craft references

Use the source that answers the current question, rather than loading a bibliography into every task.

### Light, camera, and material

- Fil Hunter, Steven Biver, and Paul Fuqua, *Light: Science & Magic* — practical reflection, diffusion, and lighting for objects.
- Matt Pharr, Wenzel Jakob, and Greg Humphreys, *Physically Based Rendering* — light transport and material rendering reference: <https://www.pbr-book.org/>.
- The camera documentation for the actual tool — practical depth, exposure, and motion decisions.

The recipes use these ideas as visual cues: a highlight can reveal curvature, a shadow can ground contact, and camera distance changes perspective. None is a prompt guarantee.

### Motion and editing

- Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* — classic animation principles such as anticipation, staging, and easing.
- Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* — editorial rhythm and the meaning of a cut.
- A current tool's official image-to-video, reference, camera-control, and duration documentation — model capabilities and syntax change faster than a static recipe can.

### Viewer understanding and persuasion

- John Sweller, “Cognitive Load During Problem Solving” — a useful reason to keep the viewer's main job clear; it does not prove a fixed composition rule.
- Joann Peck and Terry Childers, work on need for touch in consumer decisions — a useful prompt for showing scale and texture; it does not predict every response.
- Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* — an optional lens for awareness and message sophistication; audience-specific judgment still matters.

### Audio and placement

- Charles Spence, “Crossmodal Correspondences: A Tutorial Review,” *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics* (2011) — background for considering how sound and image can reinforce one another.
- Current [Meta Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/help), [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter), [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/), [Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/library), and [web performance](https://web.dev/) documentation — verify current placement, overlay, encoding, and policy details at the time of use.

Keep source claims modest. If a recipe says that an asset shows a result, the product record, source footage, measured test, or other supplied evidence must carry that claim.
