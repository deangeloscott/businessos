# Give the Voice a Point of View

Make a narrator, presenter, character, or brand voice feel intentional and suited to the message.

## Borrow this treatment

Decide what the voice knows, wants the listener to feel or do, and how close it should sound. A calm guide, precise expert, curious host, warm peer, or energetic advocate can carry the same words very differently. Let the choice show through phrasing, pace, emphasis, breath, and restraint rather than asking volume or effects to create personality on their own.

## Adapt it

Match the voice to the audience, subject, brand, and listening context. A serious explanation may need authority and room to think; a product invitation may need warmth and confidence; an interview may benefit from natural interruption and imperfection. Preserve the speaker's actual meaning and follow any disclosure, consent, pronunciation, or voice-use requirements that apply. A different voice is a creative choice, not evidence of expertise or identity.

## Example

For a technical onboarding piece, use a patient guide who names the next decision before giving the instruction. For a founder message, let the speaker's natural conviction carry the point and edit only the repetition that obscures it.

## Try it when

The words are accurate but the delivery still feels interchangeable, distant, or mismatched to the relationship the audience needs.
