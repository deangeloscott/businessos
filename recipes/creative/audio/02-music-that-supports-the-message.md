# Music That Supports the Message

Give a piece emotional shape and continuity while leaving the message easy to hear and understand.

## Borrow this treatment

Choose a mood and movement that belong to the message. Let the music establish a setting, carry a transition, or lift around a meaningful reveal; leave space for speech, important effects, and silence. A restrained bed, a pulse, a sparse texture, or a deliberate absence of music can all work. Let the arc follow the content rather than forcing every moment to swell.

## Adapt it

Match density, instrumentation, energy, and repetition to the brand, audience, placement, and duration. A product demonstration may need a quiet texture; a launch announcement may benefit from a clearer rise; a serious explanation may need almost no score. Choose music that can be licensed or otherwise used legitimately, and let the actual mix determine whether it supports or competes with the message.

## Example

Under a short feature reveal, begin with a sparse pulse and one sustained texture, thin it while the explanation is spoken, let the same motif widen briefly at the visible result, and resolve back to quiet. For a podcast intro, use a brief motif that clears before the host begins rather than running beneath every sentence.

## Try it when

The piece needs mood, continuity, or a change in energy and the music can add that without carrying a claim the words or visuals do not support.

For an accessibility-oriented reference on keeping foreground speech separate from background sound, see W3C's [Low or No Background Audio guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/low-or-no-background-audio.html). Its success criterion applies to a specific prerecorded audio-only case; use the applicable standard for the actual medium rather than treating one mix value as a universal creative rule.
