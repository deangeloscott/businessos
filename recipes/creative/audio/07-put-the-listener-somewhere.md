# Put the Listener Somewhere

Use room tone, environmental sound, and spatial contrast to make a scene, conversation, or experience feel situated.

## Borrow this treatment

Choose the smallest surrounding world that helps the listener understand the moment: a quiet room, workshop, street, kitchen, venue, landscape, or device context. Establish it briefly, keep it coherent under the voice or action, and let a change in ambience mark a meaningful move. Silence can be the clearest place when the subject needs full attention.

## Adapt it

Use a real recording when location authenticity matters, a designed environment for an illustrative scene, or a restrained room tone for a clean explanation. Keep environmental detail from masking speech or implying a location the source does not support. The same treatment can make a podcast story vivid, ground a product film, orient an audio instruction, or distinguish sections in a presentation.

## Example

Let a short workshop ambience establish a tool demonstration, then quiet it while the presenter explains the mechanism. For a travel story, allow the recurring sound of the place to return between spoken moments so the listener can follow the change in location. The BBC Academy's [sound-storytelling guide](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/academy/academyfiles/How_%20to_Tell_Stories_Using_Sound_BBC_Academy_Podcast_Transcript%20%5B23032017%5D.pdf) is a useful example of treating location sound as part of the story rather than filler.

## Try it when

The message depends on place, atmosphere, or a sense of being present, and a dry voice or isolated sound would leave the moment hard to imagine.
