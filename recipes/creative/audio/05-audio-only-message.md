# An Audio-Only Message

Make an idea, offer, story, or instruction work when there is no dependable visual channel.

## Borrow this treatment

Open with enough context to orient the listener, state one problem or desired outcome, develop it with a concrete example or piece of evidence, and finish with a clear next step or satisfying close. Use voice as the spine and add music, room tone, or a small sound cue only when it improves understanding or feeling. Repeat a necessary name, number, or instruction naturally rather than assuming it was seen on screen.

## Adapt it

Use this for a podcast segment, audio ad, phone or voice experience, screenless instruction, narrated case explanation, or a version of a visual campaign. Match structure and pacing to whether the listener is walking, driving, browsing, or giving full attention. Keep factual claims tied to the supplied record and provide a transcript or text companion when the context calls for one.

## Example

For a service introduction, begin with the situation the listener recognizes, explain the mechanism or benefit in plain language, give one concrete example of the experience, and say exactly what the listener can do next. For a product clip without visuals, describe the action and result rather than relying on words such as “look” or “as you can see.”

## Try it when

The communication must work through screen-off playback, audio-first distribution, hands-free use, or a listener who has no visual source.
