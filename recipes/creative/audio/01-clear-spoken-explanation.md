# Clear Spoken Explanation

Help a listener understand one idea, why it matters, and what to do with it.

## Borrow this treatment

Open with the question, situation, or useful outcome the listener already cares about. Speak in short, natural phrases. Give each idea room to land, use emphasis where the meaning changes, and support the explanation with one concrete example or visible action when another medium is present. A short pause can separate the setup from the answer more effectively than constant music or editing.

## Adapt it

Use this for narration, a presenter, a product walkthrough, a voice note, an explainer, a podcast segment, or an audio ad. Match vocabulary, warmth, pace, and authority to the listener and context. Write for the ear rather than reading a dense page aloud. If the speaker or voice is synthetic, preserve pronunciation, disclosure, and brand requirements that apply to the actual use.

## Example

For a feature walkthrough, begin with the task the user wants to complete, explain the one change that makes it easier, and let the matching screen action carry the explanation. For an audio-only segment, replace the screen action with a concrete example or short story.

## Try it when

The message depends on understanding, guidance, or trust more than on a decorative soundscape.
