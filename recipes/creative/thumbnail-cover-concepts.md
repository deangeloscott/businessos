# Thumbnail and cover concepts

> Optional craft reference. Use this when a thumbnail or cover must earn attention at browsing size while making an honest promise about the content. It is a small set of directions to borrow, not a universal click style or a guarantee of performance.

## Start with the real promise

Use the actual subject, title, audience question, and meaningful tension or outcome. Let the title and image add information rather than repeat the same words. A strong cover can invite a click through a detail, contrast, visible result, or unresolved moment; it should still match what the viewer will find.

## Four directions to borrow

| Direction | Useful when | Possible treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Intriguing detail** | One small object, expression, interface state, or material detail carries the question. | Crop close to the unusual detail and give it enough context to remain recognizable. |
| **Visible result** | The content genuinely demonstrates a changed state, finished artifact, or observable outcome. | Show the result clearly, using a real or clearly illustrative image rather than fabricating proof. |
| **Contrast** | Two states, scales, choices, or conditions make the subject immediately legible. | Put the meaningful difference in the frame; do not invent a before/after, reaction, or performance claim. |
| **Unresolved moment** | Curiosity comes from an action, decision, reveal, or consequence that the content will answer. | Freeze just before the meaningful turn, while keeping the subject and actual payoff clear enough to avoid a false promise. |

Choose one direction or combine them when the combination improves recognition. A face, object, diagram, screenshot, or place may carry the frame; there is no required subject or text treatment.

## Adapt for browsing and reuse

Check the actual platform crop and small viewing size. Keep the focal subject, silhouette, contrast, and any necessary words legible without relying on fine detail. For a recurring series, preserve a useful identity anchor while changing the content-specific question or tension so every cover does not become interchangeable.

A few meaningful variants can explore different hypotheses—detail versus contrast, result versus unresolved moment, or image-led versus title-led—not merely cosmetic color or face changes. Do not infer a metric guarantee from the number of variants. Keep text separate and editable when revision or localization is likely; baked-in text may suit one fixed cover but reduces flexibility.

Inspect the final title-image-content promise at the intended browse size.
