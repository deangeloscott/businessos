# Relevant outreach

Use this when contacting someone who has not asked for the offer yet but there is a legitimate, specific reason the message may be useful to them.

## The idea

Earn attention through relevance and permission, not a generic self-introduction. Show that the message is connected to the recipient's situation, offer one useful observation or resource, and make the next action small and optional.

## Borrowable shape

**Relevant context → useful observation or help → why it may matter to this recipient → low-friction invitation → easy decline.**

Keep the reason for contact specific and truthful. Do not pretend to have a relationship, imply research you did not perform, or turn a guessed pain point into a fact. The invitation can be a useful resource, a question, or a short conversation; it does not need to be a meeting request.

## Tiny illustrative example

**Subject: A small way to make the seasonal display easier**

I noticed your shop is preparing a seasonal display. We made a short checklist for keeping the product story, signs, and staff explanation aligned. It may be useful if that preparation is part of your current work; if not, no reply is needed.

## Adapt

Use current, permission-respecting channel practices and honor opt-out or contact rules. Lead with something useful enough to stand alone. A warm introduction can name the shared context, a cold message can lead with one concrete observation, and a high-value outreach can ask a precise question instead of requesting a meeting immediately. If the message requires a strong assumption about the recipient, ask a question or omit the assumption. Measure success by useful conversations and fit, not by sending volume alone.
