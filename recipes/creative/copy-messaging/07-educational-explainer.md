# Educational explainer

Use this when the audience needs to understand a concept, mechanism, or choice before a product, service, or recommendation will make sense.

## The idea

Start from the reader's question, give them a simple mental model, add one concrete example, and close with what the explanation lets them do. Teach the useful distinction instead of front-loading a list of features or jargon.

## Borrowable shape

**Question or misconception → plain-language model → concrete example → important boundary or exception → useful next step.**

Introduce one term at a time and let the example carry the abstraction. Use a diagram, analogy, or short sequence when it reduces the words needed. If the topic is consequential, preserve the qualifications and source the material that supports them.

## Tiny illustrative example

**What does a materials care label help you decide?**

Think of it as a short guide to the conditions the material can handle and the care that helps preserve it. It can tell you how to wash, dry, store, or clean the item; it does not promise that every stain, repair, or use condition will have the same result. Use the actual label and product guidance in the finished explanation.

## Adapt

Use the audience's starting knowledge and the decision the explanation supports. A beginner may need the analogy; an expert may need the boundary conditions and source. Stop when the reader can use the idea. Link to deeper material instead of making a short explainer carry every edge case.
