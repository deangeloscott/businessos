# Story or proof-led message

Use this when a real experience, demonstration, quote, or documented result can make the value easier to believe and remember.

## The idea

Let the evidence carry the story. Establish the situation, show the meaningful change or observation, explain what produced it, and connect it to the reader's decision without turning one example into a universal promise.

## Borrowable shape

**Situation or question → observed action or evidence → meaningful change → why it happened → who it may fit → next step.**

Use a customer story, product demonstration, expert explanation, or before-and-after only when the underlying record supports it. Preserve scope, conditions, dates, attribution, and limitations where they change interpretation. A clearly labeled illustrative scenario can teach the mechanism, but it is not proof.

## Tiny illustrative example

**A local café kept hearing the same question about the new roast.**

The barista's short explanation of origin, roast, and brewing choice gave guests a clearer way to choose. Identify what was actually observed, who said it, and the conditions before using a story publicly; this example does not claim a measured business result.

## Adapt

Lead with the part of the story that answers the audience's real doubt. Use a short quote when the person's exact words matter, and paraphrase when clarity matters more. Do not edit a testimonial into a stronger claim than the speaker made, imply causation from a correlation, or present a concept scene as a customer result.
