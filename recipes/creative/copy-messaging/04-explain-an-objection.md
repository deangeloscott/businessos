# Explain an objection

Use this when a person is interested but needs a direct answer to a concern about fit, risk, effort, price, timing, compatibility, or trust.

## The idea

Treat the objection as information about the decision, not as resistance to overpower. Answer the exact concern first, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, state the boundary or tradeoff, and leave the reader with an honest way to decide.

## Borrowable shape

**Name the concern → answer it plainly → explain the relevant condition or mechanism → show supported evidence or an honest limit → offer the next useful check.**

Use the person's language when it clarifies the question. A short comparison, demonstration, specification, example, or policy link can carry more confidence than extra reassurance. If the offer is not a fit, say what would be a better fit or what must be checked.

## Tiny illustrative example

**“Will this work with the setup we already have?”**

It works with the listed connection standard; the supplied setup guide shows the required port and the one adapter that may be needed. If your equipment uses another standard, send the model detail before ordering so compatibility can be checked. This is a fictional example; replace the compatibility statement with current documented information.

## Adapt

Use a calm explanation for a high-trust decision and a compact answer for a fast-moving placement. Put material terms where the person can find them. Do not answer an uncertainty with an invented guarantee, testimonial, number, or universal claim. The goal is a better decision, including a decision not to proceed when the fit is wrong.
