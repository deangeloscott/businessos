# Helpful follow-up

Use this after someone has shown interest, received something, or paused before acting. The goal is to make the next decision easier by giving something useful first. For a direct concern, use [Explain an objection](04-explain-an-objection.md).

## The idea

Continue the person's context instead of restarting the pitch. Add the smallest useful explanation or resource, then offer a low-friction next step. A helpful follow-up can end with no sales action when that is the honest fit.

## Borrowable shape

**Acknowledge the context → add one useful insight or resource → show why it matters → offer a proportionate next step → leave room to decline or ask more.**

Use the person's actual context where appropriate. If the next step depends on fit, price, timing, compatibility, or evidence, state what is known and what still needs checking. Keep the call to action consistent with the amount of trust the message has earned.

## Tiny illustrative example

**Subject: A simpler way to compare the service options**

Thanks for asking about the service options. The practical difference is how much of the preparation your team wants to handle day to day. This short comparison lays out the tradeoff in one view. If you share which part matters most, we can point you to the relevant option—or leave the comparison with you to decide.

Replace the question, answer, comparison, and next step with supported information from the actual conversation and offer.

## Adapt

For a post-download email, lead with the promised resource or one useful application. For a sales follow-up, make the next action easy to understand and easy to decline. Remove urgency, proof, or reassurance that the organization cannot substantiate.
