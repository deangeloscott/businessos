# Headline and opening variants

Use this when the underlying message is sound but the first line needs a few distinct ways to earn attention and orient the reader.

## The idea

Vary the entry point while keeping the same supported promise and audience. Useful openings can name the desired outcome, surface a real tension, show a meaningful contrast, answer a question, or begin with an observed detail. Generate a small set of genuinely different angles instead of rewriting one sentence with cosmetic synonyms.

## Borrowable shape

**One message → several entry angles → a short reason each may fit → selected opening → supporting line and next action.**

Keep the body honest when the headline becomes more forceful. A curiosity opening should resolve into a useful answer; a bold outcome should be bounded by its actual conditions; a question should be one the audience might really ask.

## Tiny illustrative example

For the same fictional insulated-bottle message, possible openings are:

- **Outcome:** Keep your drink cold through the afternoon.
- **Tension:** Tired of lukewarm drinks halfway through the day?
- **Mechanism:** Double-wall insulation slows the exchange with the outside air.
- **Question:** How long should your drink stay cold on the route you take?

These are angle examples, not tested winners or claims about a real product.

## Adapt

Choose the angle that matches awareness, placement, and trust. Use customer language when it is supported and permitted; write original copy when it is not. Pair each headline with enough context to prevent a misleading impression, then let the model or user choose, combine, or discard the variants.
