# Second opinions from several models

Use this when a decision matters enough to hear more than one view, and the organization can reach more than one AI app or model.

## The idea

Different models weigh the same facts differently. Because every connected app reads the same saved memory, the same question can go to each of them without retelling the business. The value is in where they disagree, and in what evidence would settle it.

## Borrowable shape

**One question → the same question to each model → where they agree → where they split and why → what evidence would settle it → the decision, saved with its basis.**

Write the question once, with the choice and the deadline. Ask each model in its own chat, without showing it the others' answers first. Ask each for a position in one line, the saved evidence it relied on, and what it is unsure of. Then compare.

## Tiny illustrative example

*"Should we raise the price of our main product by 5% this month?"* Model one says hold, citing a rival's recent cut. Model two says raise, citing four weeks of rising pre-orders. Model three says raise for wholesale only. They agree the rival's cut targets walk-in trade. They split on how price-sensitive regulars are, and the saved evidence on that is thin. This example is fictional.

## Adapt

Three views are usually enough. A split is a result, not a failure: it shows which fact is missing. Save the outcome as a decision with what it was based on and the open question, so the next session does not start over. Do not average the answers, count votes as evidence, or present agreement between models as proof. If only one model is available, ask it for the strongest case for and against instead.
