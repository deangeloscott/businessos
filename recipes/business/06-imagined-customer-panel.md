# An imagined customer panel

Use this when the organization wants to rehearse how different customers might react to a page, an offer or a message before it goes out.

## The idea

A panel of imagined customers is a way to find objections early. It is not research and it predicts nothing. Its value is in the questions it raises, which real evidence can then answer.

## Borrowable shape

**Build a few customers from the saved audience segments and real customer language → show each the draft → collect first reaction, what is unclear, what would stop them → group the objections → list which ones saved evidence already answers and which need real checking.**

Base each imagined customer on what is saved: their needs, the words they use, what has stopped people like them before. Six to ten voices that differ in a meaningful way are more useful than fifty that agree.

## Tiny illustrative example

*A weekend regular says the page never mentions pick-up times. A commuter cannot tell whether pre-orders work on weekdays. A cafe owner wants wholesale prices and finds none. Two of three objections are answered by saved facts that the page leaves out.* This example is fictional, and so is every reaction in it.

## Adapt

Label the output as imagined everywhere it appears, including file names and headings. Do not save a reaction as a customer finding, quote it as feedback, or count how many "liked" something. Follow it with a real check where one is cheap: a question to five customers, a look at existing reviews, or a small test with a measured result.
