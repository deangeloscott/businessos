# A portrait of the business

Use this when the organization wants one image that shows what it is, drawn from everything saved about it.

## The idea

A portrait is a picture of character, not a chart. It works when someone who knows the business recognises it at once. Because it is drawn from saved memory, it gets richer as the memory does, and making it again later shows how the business has changed.

## Borrowable shape

**What the business makes and for whom → the two or three things that set it apart → its current goals and what it is working on → one scene that holds all of that → the image, with a line saying what each element stands for.**

Read the description, offers, audiences, goals and the best-supported findings. Choose a few true, specific details over many generic ones. Describe the scene in words first and check it against the record, then make the image with whatever image tool is available.

## Tiny illustrative example

*A corner bakery at first light: a pre-order board filling up for Saturday, a rack of loaves with "36 hours" chalked beside it, a map on the wall with three cafes pinned, and a jar on the counter labelled with the review count.* This example is fictional.

## Adapt

Keep private details out: no customer names, revenue or anything the owner would not put in the window. A stylised or illustrated look avoids implying a photograph of a real place or person. Say the image is an illustration when it is shared. Do not draw results that have not happened, such as awards or crowds, as if they had.
