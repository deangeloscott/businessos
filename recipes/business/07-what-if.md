# A what-if before a decision

Use this when the organization wants to think a change through before making it: a price, an opening hour, a new product, a dropped one.

## The idea

A what-if lays out what would have to be true for a change to work, using the organization's own saved numbers and findings. It is reasoning, not a forecast. Its most useful output is usually the one assumption that matters most and is least known.

## Borrowable shape

**The change, stated exactly → what it affects → the saved facts that bear on it → the assumptions it rests on, each marked known, partly known or unknown → two or three plausible outcomes → the cheapest way to test the weakest assumption.**

Use recorded figures where they exist and say where they come from. Where a number is missing, give a range and say it is assumed. Show the arithmetic so the owner can change an assumption and see what moves.

## Tiny illustrative example

*Raise the main loaf from $9.00 to $9.50. Known: 47 pre-orders last Saturday, rising for four weeks. Partly known: regulars rarely mention price in reviews. Unknown: how many walk-in buyers compare with the rival now at $7.50. If no one leaves, about $23 more each Saturday; if one in ten walk-ins leaves, less than today. Test: raise the price for pre-orders only for two weeks.* This example is fictional.

## Adapt

Keep the result as a hypothesis with its assumptions and what would change it. Do not present a modelled outcome as an expected result, or pick the scenario the owner hopes for. When the test has run, record what was measured and compare it with what was assumed.
