# External Ecosystem Intelligence and Learning

AURA may learn from the external world, but external popularity, repetition, or model confidence must never become organizational truth by accident. This policy governs evidence and Learning from tactics, claims, events, research, platform changes, customer signals, competitor movements, and other outside developments.

## Division of responsibility

- **AURA** preserves organization-owned sources/evidence, provenance, freshness/history, observations, supported interpretations, outcomes, and Learning when they have durable value.
- **The active model/user** performs semantic interpretation, chooses appropriate research depth and sources, recognizes equivalent/conflicting claims, reasons about applicability, and decides what the evidence justifies.
- **The host/harness** supplies actual retrieval tools, APIs, browsers, models, scheduling, concurrency, credentials, and execution machinery.
- **Deterministic AURA helpers** handle mechanical IDs, hashes, exact references, timestamps, schema/reference validation, isolation, and safe persistence. They do not decide semantic equivalence, applicability, truth, or business value.

AURA may remember monitoring/cadence intent when useful. Only the external runtime that actually creates or observes recurring execution can claim that automation is active.

## Discovery rules

1. Use the evidence sources and discovery breadth that can materially improve the current decision. A known-source list is a useful seed, not a mandatory search universe or fixed quota.
2. Discovery should not depend only on literal keywords when semantic variants, entities, mechanisms, citation trails, communities, primary research, or authoritative sources would materially improve recall or confidence.
3. Search results, snippets, feeds, previews, and AI summaries are discovery leads until the underlying material is inspected/preserved strongly enough for the claim being made. Follow `core/policies/research-evidence.md`.
4. Preserve original publication/event time separately from retrieval time when freshness matters. Prefer the earliest traceable originating evidence when lineage affects interpretation.
5. Do not copy an unnecessary archive of the web. Preserve the smallest evidence, reference, hash, excerpt, screenshot/frame, or durable external pointer needed to support material organizational conclusions.

## Source attention is not source truth

SourceProfiles may help future work remember where useful evidence has historically been found. Authority is claim-type-specific: a source can be authoritative for its own policy and weak evidence for causal performance claims.

Follower count, engagement, prestige, virality, repeated citation, or confident language do not establish that a claim is true. Historical source usefulness may guide attention, but each material claim still needs evidence appropriate to what is being asserted.

## Independence, corroboration, and contradiction

- Trace whether apparently separate reports originate from one experiment, dataset, announcement, anecdote, or copied source.
- Repetition of one lineage is not independent replication.
- Distinguish independent support from echoes, commentary, extensions, and direct contradiction.
- For consequential claims, look for disconfirming evidence, failed replications, counterexamples, methodological criticism, alternative explanations, and current authoritative guidance when those could materially change the conclusion.
- Preserve unresolved contradictions instead of forcing a binary answer when evidence is mixed or insufficient.

## Freshness and novelty

Freshness is mechanism-specific. Rapidly changing platform behavior may decay quickly; durable customer/business mechanisms may decay slowly; formal rules may remain authoritative until amended or superseded.

Preserve evidence date, retrieval date, relevant platform/market/version context, and current-versus-superseded state when those details materially affect future use. Compare new evidence with existing organizational knowledge so renamed or newly viral old ideas are not automatically treated as novel.

Novelty changes investigation value; it does not change truth.

## Applicability and business relevance

External evidence can establish what happened elsewhere. It does not establish that the active organization has the same audience, assets, economics, capabilities, market, customer behavior, or expected outcome.

Before expensive research, testing, or adoption, the active model/user should consider whatever is material to the decision, such as:
- current Objective and likely business value;
- mechanism and applicability conditions;
- evidence strength, independence, freshness, and causal ambiguity;
- implementation cost, reversibility, actual policy/legal/customer constraints, and downside;
- what new information a test would provide.

These are reasoning considerations, not deterministic AURA gates.

## From signal to Learning

Use the narrowest truthful state that future work benefits from. A common progression is:

`SourceRecord -> Observation -> Insight -> Opportunity / Experiment -> OutcomeEvaluation -> Learning`

but these are semantic states, not mandatory lifecycle stages. Skip objects that do not carry real organizational meaning.

A promising uncertain claim normally remains an Observation, candidate Insight, hypothesis, or narrow experimental Learning until stronger evidence supports more. Negative, null, contradictory, and superseding evidence should remain inspectable when materially relevant.

External discovery does **not** automatically establish an active-business fact, authorize an outward claim, prove a tactic will work, or justify changing AURA product source. If the user/model chooses to act, the active host performs the real work within the user's request and actual external constraints. Persist a `DecisionRecord`, `WorkRequest`, `ChangeEvent`, `VerificationRecord`, Experiment, OutcomeEvaluation, or Learning only when that object's meaning actually occurred and future organizational work benefits from remembering it.
