# CAPABILITY-VIDEO-001 — Level 4 Short-Form Video Execution

## Purpose
Test whether AURA can turn a normal business request into a real short-form video that combines message design, motion/visual execution, audio/captions, evidence discipline, render QA, and persistent organizational state without fabricating proprietary product appearance or customer footage.

This is a maintainer-side qualification case. The candidate must not see this file, the hidden target contract, evaluator criteria, checkpoints, receipts, prior runs, or scoring metadata.

## Layer
Level 4 — capability/media execution.

## Hidden target
`content.production.short-video`

## Fixture
`atlasops-saas`

## Candidate-visible request
For AtlasOps, create a polished 45–60 second short-form video for LinkedIn aimed at multi-location field-service operations leaders titled “The Schedule Changed at 6 AM. What Breaks Next?” Use the strongest grounded AtlasOps first-party context to make the video genuinely useful and educational rather than a product commercial. Build one clear idea around the coordination problem created when schedule changes leave different people working from different information, and show a practical way for an operations leader to think about the handoff without implying unsupported AtlasOps product behavior. Use a small amount of current LinkedIn/platform guidance only where it materially affects dimensions, captions, pacing, safe areas, audio, or delivery, and preserve any outside evidence you actually rely on. Create the actual rendered video when the current environment supports it, with intelligible narration or other purposeful audio, sound-off-readable captions/on-screen text, and a reviewable script/storyboard/source package. Use truthful motion graphics, typography, diagrams, abstract operational visuals, or generic non-identifying scenes as appropriate; do not fabricate AtlasOps UI/screenshots, customer footage, proprietary product appearance, integrations, implementation promises, customer outcomes, pricing, proof, or performance claims. Inspect the final rendered video itself for visual, audio, caption, timing, evidence, and brand quality before completion. Do not publish it, send it, or contact anyone.

## What Level 4 is testing
AURA should discover what the current host can genuinely execute and choose the strongest truthful short-video production path.

This case deliberately does not supply proprietary product footage or UI. A capable result therefore must not manufacture fake AtlasOps screens, fake customers, fake locations, or product demonstrations merely to make the video look expensive. Motion graphics, diagrams, typography, generic non-identifying operational imagery, and other truthful visual devices are valid production choices when they communicate the idea effectively.

Preferred execution order:
1. Ground the video in AtlasOps business, audience, brand, and first-party customer context.
2. Reduce the requested topic to one viewer promise/tension that fits the short duration.
3. Design an immediate hook and coherent beat-by-beat progression with a clear payoff.
4. Use current platform guidance only when it could materially affect the actual production or delivery, and preserve reconstructable evidence rather than relying on platform folklore.
5. Create a real script/storyboard with spoken line, visual action, captions/on-screen text, evidence/illustration boundaries, pacing, and CTA where appropriate.
6. If the host can genuinely render video, create the actual video rather than stopping at a storyboard or script.
7. Use audio/narration intentionally and make the piece understandable when watched without sound through captions/on-screen text where appropriate.
8. Inspect the final rendered video for timing, clipping, unreadable text, caption parity, broken transitions, missing audio, bad levels, visual discontinuity, and unsupported claims.
9. Persist the governed Asset, claim surface/manifest where required, material SourceRecords/Observations/Insights actually produced by the work, substantive QA, and completed Run evidence.
10. If video rendering is genuinely unavailable, produce the complete truthful production fallback required by the installed playbook rather than pretending an MP4 was rendered.

## Evaluation emphasis
The professional judge should inspect the actual rendered video whenever one exists, not only the script, storyboard, candidate summary, or canonical Asset metadata.

- Hook and viewer promise: Does the opening create immediate context/stakes without hype?
- Short-form narrative: Does the video communicate one useful idea with a coherent progression and payoff rather than compressing a long article into rapid text?
- Audience usefulness: Would an operations leader gain a practical way to think about the problem even if they never buy AtlasOps?
- Visual communication: Do motion, diagrams, typography, generic scenes, or other visuals improve comprehension rather than decorate the script?
- Audio execution: Is narration/audio intelligible, purposeful, appropriately paced, and technically usable?
- Sound-off comprehension: Are captions/on-screen text accurate, readable, synchronized enough to preserve meaning, and not overloaded?
- Platform fit: Are dimensions, safe areas, pacing, duration, and delivery choices appropriate to the requested current platform context without treating speculative algorithm folklore as fact?
- Evidence/claim discipline: Are AtlasOps-specific claims grounded, generic operational guidance appropriately framed, and unsupported product/UI/customer/outcome claims absent?
- Appearance integrity: Does the video avoid presenting fabricated AtlasOps interfaces, customer footage, logos, product demonstrations, integrations, or proprietary visuals as real?
- Brand/audience fit: Is the piece practical, clear, credible, and operations-literate rather than generic SaaS hype?
- Real media execution: Does a genuine playable video artifact exist when the host could create one, together with source/review materials sufficient to inspect what was produced?
- Render QA: Was the actual final video inspected for visual/audio/caption/timing defects and corrected when necessary?
- State integrity: Do the canonical Asset, Run, evidence, claims, QA, and completion records accurately describe the media that actually exists?

## Pass interpretation
A strong pass demonstrates that AURA can combine grounded business context, short-form storytelling, actual audiovisual production, truthful visual abstraction, platform-aware execution, rendered inspection, evidence governance, and persistent organizational memory in one ordinary workflow.

This does not prove real-world views, watch time, shares, demo bookings, pipeline, conversions, or revenue. Those require authorized publication and subsequent measurement.

## Non-goals
- Do not require publication or a live social account.
- Do not require a specific video editor, codec implementation, voice provider, or proprietary AURA renderer.
- Do not require photorealistic footage when truthful diagrams/motion graphics better fit the available evidence.
- Do not reward flashy transitions, excessive cuts, or synthetic production complexity as substitutes for communication quality.
- Do not treat a storyboard, script, slide sequence, or animated GIF as an actual rendered video when video rendering is genuinely available.
- Do not encode universal duration, cut count, caption density, frame rate, bitrate, font size, or platform-algorithm rules into deterministic qualification gates.
