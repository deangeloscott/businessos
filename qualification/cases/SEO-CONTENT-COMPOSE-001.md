# SEO-CONTENT-COMPOSE-001 — SEO Intelligence → Requirements → Article

Status: planned

Qualification layer: 3 — composition quality

Benchmark business: AtlasOps

## Purpose

Test whether AURA can carry current SEO/competitor evidence through an actual multi-playbook handoff rather than producing isolated good work that downstream systems rediscover or ignore.

The chain under observation is:

1. establish enough current search/competitor evidence for the defined intent;
2. form or reuse the supported organic Opportunity/Insights;
3. translate that upstream intelligence through `seo.planning.organic-content-requirements` into a complete Content Synthesis WorkRequest/requirements set;
4. execute the resulting customer-facing article/page through Content Synthesis;
5. complete the required content subcontracts/QA and persist the final Asset/Run evidence;
6. leave an appropriate measurement/recheck plan without claiming unobserved rankings, citations, traffic, leads, or revenue.

This is deliberately narrower than the full SEO/AEO domain mission. It is meant to answer one question: **does AURA's operating intelligence compound cleanly from research into requirements into production?**

## Candidate-facing request

The ordinary request is maintained in `qualification/missions/missions.json` as `COMPOSE-SEO-CONTENT-001`. The candidate must not receive this case file, evaluator rubric, hidden event ID, qualification directory, checkpoints, receipts, or scoring metadata.

## What the evaluator should inspect

### Composition integrity

- The upstream search/competitor evidence is real, current enough, and reconstructable.
- The Opportunity/Insights are materially grounded in that evidence and the AtlasOps business context.
- The SEO requirements/WorkRequest explicitly inherit the useful upstream findings rather than starting over generically.
- The final article/page visibly satisfies those requirements and does not silently discard the differentiation strategy, must-answer questions, evidence needs, CTA handoff, internal/structured/AEO requirements, or exclusions that materially matter.
- Downstream work reuses canonical state/evidence rather than multiplying duplicate Insights, Opportunities, SourceRecords, or competing ownership.
- Required Content Synthesis subcontracts and pre-publish QA examine the actual produced Asset/version.
- The final user-facing result is an actual usable article/page artifact, not a requirements document or outline presented as production.

### Professional / competitive review

Independently inspect enough of the current search field to judge whether the final asset is genuinely informed by current competitive reality. Do not require the candidate to use the evaluator's exact sources if its evidence is equally credible.

Judge the chain as a system, not merely the prose quality of the final article:

- Did research materially improve the requirements?
- Did the requirements materially improve the produced asset?
- Did the asset preserve AtlasOps's useful differentiation and factual/brand constraints?
- Would an SEO/content professional be able to use the result without rebuilding the strategy or article from scratch?
- Is the finished asset plausibly competitive for the intended search/AI-answer task, while preserving uncertainty about authority, ranking, citation, and real-world outcomes?

## Failure diagnosis

If the composition fails, classify the failure before changing AURA:

- model execution/randomness;
- missing or weak upstream evidence;
- SEO planning/handoff weakness;
- Content Synthesis execution/QA weakness;
- state reuse/semantic ownership weakness;
- missing host capability;
- fixture/test design problem;
- evaluator/judge error.

Do not add benchmark-shaped product rules. Change AURA only if the run reveals a reusable real-world operating weakness.

## Next step after this case

If this composition chain is professionally strong, move upward to broader capability/media execution or domain/cross-domain missions rather than repeatedly hardening this exact scenario.
