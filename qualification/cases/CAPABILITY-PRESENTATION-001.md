# CAPABILITY-PRESENTATION-001 — Level 4 Presentation Outcome Quality

## Purpose
Test whether a capable model/harness using AURA can turn a normal business request into a real presentation designed for a live audience journey rather than a document broken into slides.

This is a maintainer-side qualification case. The candidate must not see this file, the hidden target Workflow, evaluator criteria, checkpoints, prior runs, or scoring metadata.

## Layer
Level 4 — medium-specific outcome quality.

## Hidden target
`content.production.presentation`

## Fixture
`atlasops-saas`

## Candidate-visible request
For AtlasOps, create a polished 12–15 minute live executive briefing for multi-location field-service operations leaders titled “Growth Without Operational Chaos: Four Coordination Decisions to Make Before Your Team Outgrows Its Current Operating Model.” Make it genuinely educational and useful even to someone who never buys AtlasOps, while fitting AtlasOps’s audience and brand context. Build a clear audience journey rather than turning an article into slides. Use the strongest grounded first-party context already available for AtlasOps and, where current outside evidence would materially improve the briefing, use a small credible research sample and preserve the evidence. Create the actual presentation in a usable presentation format when the current environment supports it, with presenter notes separated from visible slide copy, plus a rendered PDF or complete slide-preview set for review. Use diagrams, charts, examples, or other visuals when they improve understanding rather than filling slides with paragraphs. Do not invent AtlasOps product capabilities, integrations, implementation promises, customer outcomes, pricing, proof, or performance claims. Do not publish it, send it, or contact anyone.

## What Level 4 is testing
The candidate should use AURA for relevant organizational memory and presentation operating knowledge, while the active model/harness owns actual research, file creation, rendering, and tool choice.

The case intentionally tests a live briefing rather than a social carousel. The candidate should make decisions about pacing, section structure, slide count, speaker notes, evidence density, visual explanation, and closing action based on the audience and 12–15 minute setting rather than copying a generic deck template.

Preferred work pattern:
1. Ground the briefing in AtlasOps business, audience, brand, and first-party context.
2. Define the audience setting, learning/decision objective, duration, and expected prior knowledge.
3. Build a coherent narrative arc around audience questions and decisions rather than source-document headings.
4. Use current outside research only when it could materially improve or substantiate the briefing, and preserve reconstructable evidence rather than relying on unsupported generalizations.
5. Give each slide one main communication job; use charts, diagrams, examples, visual metaphors, or sparse text according to that job.
6. Keep presenter notes separate from audience-visible copy.
7. When the environment can create a presentation file, create the real editable presentation plus a rendered PDF or slide-preview set and inspect the actual render.
8. Inspect the final slides for overflow, missing glyphs, chart/data issues, spacing, hierarchy, contrast, source/render parity, and unsupported audience-facing claims; correct material problems when possible.
9. Preserve the finished presentation or useful durable reference/provenance/evidence in AURA when future organizational work would materially benefit.
10. If presentation creation/rendering is genuinely unavailable, state the external limitation truthfully. A detailed production-ready deck specification may still be useful fallback work, but it must not be represented as the requested finished presentation.

## Evaluation emphasis
The professional judge should distinguish presentation thinking from attractive slide decoration and keep host capability separate from AURA quality.

- **Audience journey:** Does the deck create a coherent 12–15 minute progression with a clear beginning, development, synthesis, and close?
- **Presentation-native structure:** Does each slide have one main job, or is the deck a document/transcript chopped into boxes?
- **Presenter support:** Are speaker notes useful and clearly separated from visible slide content?
- **Visual explanation:** Are diagrams, charts, examples, or other visuals used when they improve comprehension, with data and labels accurate and legible?
- **Viewing-distance readability:** Can the rendered slides be understood at normal presentation scale without dense paragraphs, tiny labels, clipping, or overcrowding?
- **Evidence discipline:** Are material claims supported by first-party context or reconstructable current evidence, with uncertainty qualified appropriately?
- **Brand/audience fit:** Does the briefing sound practical, clear, credible, and operations-literate for multi-location field-service leaders rather than generic SaaS marketing?
- **Commercial restraint:** Is AtlasOps positioned appropriately without converting the educational briefing into unsupported product claims or a disguised sales brochure?
- **Real media execution:** Does an actual usable presentation artifact exist when the environment could create one, together with a truthful rendered review surface?
- **Final-output inspection:** Was the actual final render reviewed and corrected where materially necessary?
- **Durable continuity:** If the presentation will matter later, does AURA accurately preserve the useful Asset/reference/provenance/evidence without requiring a Run, QA object, or completion-evidence record merely to prove the work happened?

## Pass interpretation
A strong pass demonstrates that AURA's memory and operating knowledge materially support narrative design, business context, evidence discipline, and continuity while the model/harness performs the actual presentation work.

This does not prove that the briefing will generate pipeline, demo bookings, sales, or audience engagement after delivery. Those require real-world use and measurement.

## Non-goals
- Do not require publication, distribution, or a live audience.
- Do not require Microsoft PowerPoint specifically if another interoperable editable format satisfies the real need.
- Do not require a proprietary AURA presentation renderer.
- Do not test generic slide rendering or file creation as an AURA-owned capability.
- Do not require a Run, QA object, capability record, completion-evidence profile, or approval artifact.
- Do not reward slide count, animation volume, or decorative complexity as substitutes for communication effectiveness.
- Do not require current external research when the supplied first-party context is sufficient for a particular point.
- Do not encode a universal slide count, font size, chart count, or presentation formula into deterministic qualification gates.
