- diagnose the bottleneck from available first-party/business/site evidence instead of assuming the cause;
- use local search, competitor, customer, or market evidence only where it materially improves the diagnosis or intervention;
- distinguish traffic-volume problems, intent/targeting problems, trust/proof problems, offer/friction problems, and conversion-path problems when relevant;
- identify the highest-value justified intervention rather than produce a broad generic audit;
- create the actual implementation-ready work that is feasible in the environment, such as page/copy/content/CTA/offer changes, instead of stopping at advice;
- respect local-service credibility, geography, service realities, customer concerns, and conversion behavior;
- avoid fabricated rankings, calls, leads, conversion lift, or competitor performance;
- leave a practical measurement/recheck plan for the proposed change;
- preserve only useful durable business meaning that should help later work.
