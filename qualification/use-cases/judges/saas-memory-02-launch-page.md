# Expected outcome

Judge whether a fresh model session actually benefits from the durable organizational understanding created earlier, rather than re-deriving everything from scratch or ignoring prior work.

A strong result should:

- correctly recover and use the previously supported customer segment, problems, desired outcomes, objections, and positioning implications when they remain current;
- avoid unnecessary rediscovery or duplicate research unless important information is genuinely missing or stale;
- turn that understanding into a finished, persuasive, factually supportable launch-page draft;
- preserve AtlasOps-specific differentiation, proof constraints, customer language, and an appropriate CTA/value path;
- avoid silently treating prior inference as stronger fact than it was;
- update or add durable meaning only where the new work creates something worth carrying forward.

A strong page with no meaningful use of prior organizational memory should score lower on continuity/state use than an equally strong page that compounds prior work appropriately.
